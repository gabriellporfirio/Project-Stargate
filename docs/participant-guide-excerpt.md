# Hackathon participant guide: owner-supplied excerpt

**Source:** the event's Participant Guide on the Cerebral Valley event page, supplied by the owner on 31 Oct 2026 as seven page PDFs plus a pasted copy of the event overview. Claude couldn't open the page itself (the site blocks automated reading), so this is "owner-supplied", not independently checked. The quotes below are verbatim. The guide says the Official Rules are a separate document that participants accept. It isn't attached here.

## Problem statements (section 1)
> Your project must build towards one of the following problem statements:
>
> **Build for Your World** - Bring Meta's models into your domain. Solve a problem you know firsthand, free up time and mental space, and make something possible that wasn't before.
>
> **Push the Frontier** - Use Meta's models to tackle challenges in software, robotics, manufacturing, energy, materials and beyond. Give a machine a new capability, accelerate a scientific experiment, or unlock a better way to understand and control a physical system. Show us what becomes possible when these models meet the real world.
>
> Your project must incorporate Meta's models. Use the Muse Spark series, Muse Wonder, Muse Image Muse Voice Transcribe and SAM 3.1 through Meta Model API, run Meta open weights models Muse Glimmer and Muse Spark Open, and code faster with Muse Code coding agent.

Track descriptions (event overview): "Indie developer track: For individual builders and students. Judged on creativity, technical ambition, and whether it works."

## Schedule (section 3)
> Please note: All times are in Eastern Time (ET).
>
> Tue, Nov 3: Last full day for submission questions and technical troubleshooting.
>
> Wed, Nov 4 | 9:00 PM: Final office hours in Discord; Submissions due via the CV platform.
>
> Thu, Nov 5 – Sun, Nov 8: Judging Period
>

The event overview gives the end as "Nov 5, 2:00 AM (GMT)", which matches 9:00 PM Eastern Standard Time on 4 Nov.

Office hours: "Daily channel office hours in Discord from 10-11 AM ET" on weekdays, in #office-hours.

## Rules (section 4)
> **Open source:** Repositories **must be public**.
>
> **Demo requirements:** Your demo **must only highlight the specific features, code, and functionality that you built during the Hackathon**. Judges must be able to clearly identify what was created during the Hackathon. Failure to clearly identify your original contributions will result in immediate disqualification at Judges sole discretion.
>
> **New work only:** What you present at this Hackathon must have been built during the event. This should be clear in your submissions.
>
> **Banned projects:** Projects will be **disqualified** if they: violate legal, ethical, or platform policies, use code, data, or assets you do not have the rights to.

Sample anti-projects (to avoid): "Any project using AI to generate and give medical advice …; Basic RAG Applications; Simple Image Analyzers; simple "AI for Education" Chatbots; AI Job Application Screener; AI Nutrition Coach; Personality Analyzers; Sports analyzers or coaches; Anything political or involving war".

## Meta credits (section 5)
> As an accepted participant, you get $150 in Meta Model API credits. … Claim by Wed Nov 4 at 9:00 PM Eastern Time. … In order to use your credits & make an API call, a credit card must be applied to your account. … You can set up a "Spend Limit" notification on the "Billing" page.

## Submission (section 7)
> Solo participants, teams and startups must submit here by November 4 at 9:00 PM EST. … Late submissions won't be considered.
>
> Please double-check that your repository is accessible publicly, your video link is accessible, and everyone who worked on the project is listed on the submission page.
>
> Please note: Your submission will require a **three minute demo video** highlighting the specific features, code, and functionality you built during the Hackathon. This video will also be used as one of the primary artifacts Judges use to judge your project, along with your code and live product.

## Judging (section 8) and prizes (section 9)
Same as [the earlier excerpt](hackathon-rules-excerpt.md): Stage One pass/fail on theme fit and use of Meta models; Stage Two rubric (Impact 30%, Use of Meta AI tooling 25%, Technical depth 20%, Demo 25%); Stage Three, the top 5 per track are judged on their videos live. Indie prizes: $150K, $100K, $75K, $50K, $25K.

Bonus prizes ($50K in total), quoted from the table:
- **Most Viral Project ($20,000):** "Post your project demo on X and submit the post link through the hackathon platform." Judged on engagement up to 8 Nov.
- **Best Build on Meta Open Weight Models ($10,000):** "The best project built using Muse Glimmer or Muse Spark Open, served on participants' own hardware or through partner-provided inference." The partner "verifies that the project runs on an eligible Meta open-weight model."
- **Community Vote ($10,000):** most votes in the public gallery.
- **Best Agentic Use-Case ($5,000):** "the strongest use of AI agents to complete meaningful tasks, make decisions, or coordinate multi-step workflows."
- **Best Creative Build ($5,000):** built with Muse Image or Muse Wonder.
